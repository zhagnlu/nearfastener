Socket Cap, Button, Low and Countersunk Heads: How to Choose

At a glance

Compare head clearance, tool access and mounting geometry before specifying a screw for factory assembly.

Start with space and tool access

Compare the head diameter and height with the available envelope and tool access. A socket cap head, button head and low head solve different clearance problems. Check the actual product drawing instead of treating all screws with the same thread as interchangeable.

Sources: Bossard — low-head screw loadability

Match the head to the seating surface

A countersunk head needs a matching countersink; a flat-bearing head needs its specified seating surface. Confirm the head angle, hole preparation and length convention in the drawing. A flush appearance alone does not establish that the joint is correctly specified.

Sources: Bossard — low-head screw loadability

Review loadability before substitution

Some low-head and countersunk designs have reduced loadability because of head geometry. Do not transfer torque or load assumptions from a conventional cap screw without checking the applicable product standard. ISO 10642 explicitly covers countersunk socket screws with reduced loadability.

Sources: Bossard — low-head screw loadability · ISO 10642 — countersunk screws with reduced loadability
